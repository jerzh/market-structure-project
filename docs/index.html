Does concentration eat the paycheck?

Every dot is a U.S. industry at the finest public detail (6-digit NAICS, 2022 Economic Census). Horizontal: how much of the industry's revenue goes to its four largest firms. Vertical: labor's share of the value the industry adds (payroll ÷ value added; switch to payroll ÷ revenue for exact 6-digit detail). Click a dot for its 1987–2025 labor-share history, its 2017→2022 drift, and where its jobs sit today (QCEW, 2026 Q1).

Sources, definitions and caveats
  • Concentration — U.S. Census Bureau, Economic Census 2022 and 2017, tables EC2200SIZECONCEN / EC1700SIZECONCEN: share of sales, shipments or revenue accounted for by the largest 4/8/20/50 firms; HHI published for manufacturing only. Agriculture (11) and management of companies (55) are not covered.
  • Labor share of value added — Census value added from EC2231BASIC is exact for about 350 manufacturing 6-digit industries; elsewhere this uses BEA compensation ÷ value added for the finest BEA industry in the 71-industry GDP-by-industry table containing the NAICS code, so non-manufacturing 6-digit industries inherit their parent's value and appear as vertical stripes. BEA compensation includes benefits whereas Census payroll excludes them, so the two are not strictly comparable.
  • Payroll share — the second Y option: annual payroll ÷ sales/receipts/revenue from the same tables. It is a revenue denominator, not value added, so capital- and materials-intensive industries look low; compare within a sector or over time rather than across very different industries. A few industries exceed 100% (e.g. general warehousing) because Census revenue excludes in-house logistics operations of retailers.
  • BLS history — BLS Industry Productivity program, 1987–2025. Where BLS publishes "labor share" (L03, compensation ÷ value of production, manufacturing) it is used; elsewhere compensation ÷ sectoral output (or ÷ value added at the sector level) is computed. The series is matched at the finest NAICS level BLS publishes, which is noted in the panel. Its level is not comparable to the Census payroll share: compensation includes benefits and self-employed labor, and for wholesale and retail trade BLS measures output as sales margin (sales less cost of goods resold), not sales — so trade industries show a much higher BLS ratio.
  • Jobs today — BLS QCEW, 2026 Q1 (released Aug 2026), private ownership. Geographic concentration is the Herfindahl of March employment across counties, computed only where disclosed counties cover ≥70% of national employment (6-digit where possible, else the 4-digit parent).
  • "Well-known large employers" in the panel is a hand-curated, indicative list of major firms operating in each 6-digit industry. It is not from Census or BLS — the identities of the top-4 firms behind CR4 are confidential — and has not been verified against employment counts.
  • NAICS 2017→2022 codes are matched by identical code; industries redefined between vintages show no drift. Census imputation/suppression flags are shown in the panel where present. Correlations shown are descriptive, not causal.
